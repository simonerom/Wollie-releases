Ambrogio

Hey, this is Ambrogio.

I built Ambrogio for my own family, because I wanted an assistant that lives in our house instead of in somebody's cloud. It thinks with Claude, remembers what you tell it, tracks your diet, keeps your projects and documents in order, reads your calendar and email, and answers when you say “Hey Siri”. Start with it on your iPhone. No account to create.

Put a Raspberry Pi on your shelf and Ambrogio moves in: it reads the family's email, keeps the house's calendar, drives the home automation, and keeps working while your phone is in your pocket — and everything it knows stays on that box, which you can unplug. On the phone alone, bring your own Claude subscription and it answers straight from Anthropic; use a beta tester invite instead and your questions pass through a server I run — the privacy page says exactly what that changes.

It's an open beta, and I'm looking for people who'd like to help make it more useful.

Privacy

No cloud account. And I name every place your words go.

With a box of your own: your messages, your mail, your calendar, what it remembers — all of it on the Pi on your shelf, which you can unplug. Nothing comes back to me either: no telemetry, no analytics, no crash reports. Some things do leave — the three below are the ones that matter most, and the privacy page lists every one of them, including the sources Ambrogio looks things up in and the accounts you choose to connect.

Claude, to think

What you ask, and whatever it needs to answer. Sent by the box, on your own subscription, under your own terms with Anthropic.

A notification with nothing in it

To ring your phone, Apple and Expo are told only a category: “New message”, “Heads up”, “Urgent”. The words stay on your box until you open the app.

Telegram, if you connect it

Those messages cross Telegram's servers like any other chat. Entirely optional — use the app and the console instead and Telegram is never involved.

Without a box, it is not the same

The iPhone beta works without a Pi, and then there is no box to keep anything. Unless you supply your own Anthropic key, what you ask goes to ai-gateway.sromano.com — a server I run — and from there to Anthropic. It holds the conversation so it can carry it on, and counts what each phone uses. The app never sends your name — only the phone model, the language and a code it made up. If I invited you personally, the label I typed when inviting you is on your row, and that is often a first name. Ask and your token is revoked; put in your own key, or set up a box, and this server is out of the path entirely.

The box

A Raspberry Pi on your shelf. Four steps, about a minute of your time.

You'll need a Raspberry Pi 4 or 5, a microSD card, and a computer to write it with. Writing the card takes about ten minutes and runs unattended — your part is about a minute.

Start here — write a card (macOS & Linux)
curl -LO https://github.com/simonerom/Ambrogio-releases/raw/main/tools/flash_box.py sudo python3 flash_box.py --flash

One command downloads the latest image, flashes the card you pick, and writes your claim code. On Windows, or if you'd rather use a graphical tool, the flashing guide has the Raspberry Pi Imager route too.

1

Run the script

The command above downloads the latest image, asks which removable disk is your SD card, and flashes it after a confirmation. No separate imager needed. (On Windows, or if you prefer a GUI, use Raspberry Pi Imager — the guide covers it.)

2

Print the code sheet

The same run writes a one-off claim code onto the card and hands you a sheet to print — the code, the box's Wi-Fi name, and a QR. It's your only copy, so print it and keep it. The code is what proves the box is yours when you claim it in the app.

3

Boot the Pi, get it online

Put the card in, power it on, give it a few minutes. Then get it on your network: plug in an Ethernet cable, or let the app set up Wi-Fi for it (you'll scan the QR from your sheet).

4

Claim it from the app

The app finds the box on your network and asks you to claim it. Type or scan your code to prove the box is yours, then give it a Claude subscription token so it can think — and say hello.

Why a code at all?

A fresh box announces itself on your network, and whoever claims it first becomes its owner — with access to the family's email, a Claude token, and an assistant that acts on their behalf. The code proves you're the person physically holding the box, so ownership can't go to whoever reaches it first on the network. Lost the sheet? Pull the card, write a fresh code onto it with python3 flash_box.py --force-rekey, and put it back — your data stays put.
