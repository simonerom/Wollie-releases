Ambrogio

Privacy

Ambrogio runs on a small computer you own, in your house. There is no account to create and no dashboard in someone else's data centre. This page says exactly what leaves your house, and why — including the parts that do.

The short version

Your data lives on your box. The developer of Ambrogio operates no server, has no account system, and receives nothing — no telemetry, no crash reports, no analytics, no copy of your messages. There is nowhere for us to look, which is a stronger promise than a policy saying we won't.

Three things do leave your house, all of them because you asked for a feature that needs them. They are listed below, by name.

Where your data actually is

Everything Ambrogio knows — your messages, your email, your calendar, what it remembers about your household — is stored on the Raspberry Pi (or equivalent computer) that you flashed and plugged in. The iOS app talks to that box and to nothing else. When you are away from home it reaches the box over your own private network (Tailscale); the traffic is encrypted end to end between your phone and your box.

Deleting your data means deleting it from your box, or unplugging the box. Nobody has to approve that, and no copy survives somewhere else.

What leaves your house, and why

  • Your box → Anthropic (Claude)

    Ambrogio thinks with Claude, using your own Claude subscription or API key, which you provide during setup. When you talk to Ambrogio, the box sends the conversation to Anthropic's API to get an answer, along with whatever context that answer needs — which can include the content of an email you asked about, a file you sent, or notes it has saved for you. This is the relationship between you and Anthropic, under your own credential and their terms; the developer of Ambrogio is not a party to it and never sees the traffic. The app itself never contacts Anthropic — only your box does.

  • Your box → Expo → Apple, for push notifications

    If you turn on notifications in the app, the box asks Expo's push service to ring your phone through Apple. The notification carries no content — only a generic category such as "New message", "Heads up" or "Urgent". The real text stays on your box and the app fetches it when you open it. So the fact that Ambrogio wants your attention transits Expo and Apple; what it wants to say does not.

  • Your box → Telegram, only if you connect it

    Ambrogio can be used from Telegram. If you set that up, those messages travel through Telegram's servers like any other Telegram chat, and their privacy policy applies to them. This is optional: use the app and the web console instead and Telegram is never involved.

  • Your box → the developer

    Never. There is no such channel. The app contains no analytics SDK, no crash reporter and no tracking of any kind, and the box does not phone home. Updates are something you fetch from GitHub when you choose to.

Apple Health

If you choose to connect Apple Health, Ambrogio reads these specific things, and nothing else:

  • Active energy burned and resting energy burned, per day — so the diet feature can show what you ate against what you spent.
  • Body mass and height — so you don't have to type them.
  • Biological sex — used only in the arithmetic that turns the above into a daily figure.

Ambrogio never writes anything to Apple Health. This is off until you turn it on, iOS asks you separately before any of it is readable, and you can withdraw it at any time in Settings → Health → Data Access & Devices. Health figures are stored on your box alongside the rest of your data, are never placed in iCloud, and are never used for advertising, marketing or profiling — there is nobody here to advertise to you.

One thing to be aware of

Ambrogio's answers are produced by Claude, so if you ask it a question about your health figures, those figures go to Anthropic as part of the question — the same as any other thing you ask about. The box does its own arithmetic locally and does not send your health data anywhere on its own initiative. If you would rather it never left the box at all, use the diet page and don't ask about it in chat.

What the app keeps on your phone

The address of your box and the token that pairs your phone to it, in the iOS keychain; your own display preferences; and a cached copy of the recent conversation so the app opens instantly. Removing the app removes all of it. "Unpair this device" in the app removes the token from the box as well.

Everyone in the household

An Ambrogio box can serve several people, each with their own data and their own permissions. Whoever set the box up administers it and can see that other members exist, what they are allowed to do, and anything explicitly shared with them — they cannot read another member's private conversations or mail. If a child uses Ambrogio, the same applies, and the adult who set the box up is responsible for what it is allowed to do.

Changes

If what leaves your house ever changes, this page changes with it and the date below moves. Ambrogio is open about its own workings on purpose: if you want to check any statement here, the code that does it is public.
